<original_problem_statement>
The user wants to build a Grand Theft Auto (GTA) style GPS RPG game.

**PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS (from initial state and this session):**
- **Core Gameplay:** Monster battles and missions triggered by GPS location.
- **Map &amp; Theme:** A real-world map (using OpenStreetMap) that shows the player's location, enemies, missions, and dynamic shops.
- **GPS Movement:** The player's character on the map must move in real-time as the player moves in the real world.
- **Character System:** A character screen showing stats, skills, inventory, and equipable items. Players should be able to upload a custom profile picture, sell/delete items, and view their owned territories.
- **Authentication:** Players must be able to create an account (login/password) to save their character, inventory, and game progress.
- **Missions:**
    - A dedicated page/tab to track all accepted missions.
    - Missions can be accepted by multiple players simultaneously, with progress tracked individually.
    - Bounty missions should only be generated for enemies within the player's current tier.
    - Players should be able to cancel an accepted mission.
- **Economy:**
    - An item shop, item drops from battles, and dynamic, location-based gun shops on the map.
    - Territories should provide an immediate income bonus upon capture and a daily income that can be collected.
- **Multiplayer &amp; Territories:** PvP, Guilds, Leaderboards, and a Territory control system.
- **Progression &amp; Content:**
    - A 10-tier enemy system.
    - A passive health regeneration system.
    - A fitness tracker page for steps, calories, and distance. The tracker should work even if the device pedometer is unavailable.
- **UI/UX:**
    - Custom images for different enemy types should be displayed on the map and in battles.
    - Enemy level numbers must be clearly visible on the map.
    - An options screen (both on the landing page and in-game) with Mute Music and Quit Game buttons.
    - Looping background music on the intro/landing screen.
</original_problem_statement>
